
*"The body becomes matter, colour becomes presence: an essential image built on the contrast between skin, fabric and red."*

**Description of the work**

*Red* stands out within Massimo Di Stefano’s body of work for its more direct and figurative approach. The work focuses attention on the legs of a female figure and on the stark chromatic contrast between the red of the skirt and the silvery hue of the figure.

The composition deliberately eschews a detailed depiction of the body. The figure is partially isolated and transformed into an essential image, in which colour and material take on a role almost equivalent to that of form.

The red of the skirt is the work’s principal visual element: intense, solid and instantly recognisable, it becomes the focal point that most captures the viewer’s gaze. In contrast, the legs take on a metallic hue that distances them from a naturalistic representation and lends the figure an almost sculptural quality.

Particularly significant is the decision to use fabric directly in the construction of the surface. The creases and folds of the material create an irregular texture that is integrated into the depiction of the legs, whilst plaster and vinyl glues contribute to the texture of the skirt and the background.

The result is an image that lies somewhere between painting, collage and relief. The subject remains recognisable, but is filtered through the material and through a very stark chromatic reduction.

*Red* does not, therefore, seek any particular conceptual complexity. Its strength lies above all in its visual impact: **a female figure reduced to a few essential elements, in which red dominates the composition and the material alters its perception**.

**Technique and materials**

Mixed media on polystyrene, created using **paper, fabric, plaster, vinyl glues, acrylic paints and spray paints**.

The surface of the polystyrene has been modified to create different levels of texture. Plaster and vinyl adhesives form the more textured areas, whilst the fabric applied to the surface creates creases and raised areas that become an integral part of the figure.

Acrylics and spray paints complete the colour scheme, creating a contrast between the intense red of the skirt, the silvery hue of the legs and the textured background. The mixed media technique thus allows for a balance to be maintained between the figurative image and the textural composition.
