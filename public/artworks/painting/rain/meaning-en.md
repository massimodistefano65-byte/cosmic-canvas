
*"Sometimes all it takes is to freeze a moment in time to transform something that flows into a trace that endures."*

**Description of the work**

*Rain* stems from the idea of visually capturing a phenomenon that, by its very nature, is continuous and fleeting: rain. The work does not seek to depict a landscape in the rain, but rather to isolate a single moment of its movement, as if a single frame had been slowed down to the point of revealing what the eye normally perceives only as an indistinct mass of raindrops.

The surface of the canvas is built up using a rich, layered medium, in which vinyl glues, earth pigments and acrylics form an irregular and deeply textural base. Numerous vertical lines have been traced and carved into this surface, interrupting the material and creating the impression of streaks and trajectories left by the raindrops as they fall.

The vertical movement of the marks lends the work a distinctive dynamic tension. The lines appear both still and in motion: they are the result of a gesture physically etched into the material, yet they suggest something that continues to fall beyond the moment depicted.

The presence of gold introduces a fundamental shift in the perception of the image. The gilded surface reacts to light differently depending on the angle and the surroundings, producing reflections that can become much brighter than when viewed head-on. In this way, the work is not entirely static, but changes with the lighting conditions and the viewer’s position.

The green and violet hues further expand this perception, creating a chromatic contrast with the golden areas and the darker elements. The colours do not construct a precise naturalistic setting, but help to create a suspended atmosphere, in which the rain can also be perceived as an emotional experience.

*Rain* can therefore be viewed on multiple levels. It may simply evoke rain and its movement, but it may also suggest a more inward state: something that passes through a moment in our lives, leaving traces that remain even after the phenomenon that generated them has ended.

Time thus becomes an implicit component of the work. A movement that is normally too rapid to be observed is slowed down, etched and transformed into matter. The rain is not merely depicted: it is **held within the moment**.

**Technique and materials**

Mixed media on canvas, created using **vinyl glues, earth pigments and acrylic paints**, with the addition of gold paint.

The material is worked and incised to create an uneven surface, in which the carved lines produce the characteristic vertical streaks that evoke falling rain. The varying depths of the surface interact with the light and accentuate the perception of movement.

The gold colour also takes on a particular perceptual function: its ability to reflect light alters the appearance of the surface depending on the lighting and the viewing angle, making the work visually changeable.
