*"Sometimes a work of art is simply born out of a fascination. Not everything needs to be explained: some images simply ask to be painted."*



**Description of the work**

*The Indian* dates from the early years of my exploration of painting. Created in 2009, it is a work born of a direct fascination with the figure of the Indian and with what this image has always evoked in me: wisdom, knowledge, inner strength and a deeper connection with nature.

The figure is depicted using a deliberately simple and minimalist style. There is no intention to construct a complex scene or to tell a specific story. The work focuses attention on the face and the presence of the figure, allowing just a few elements to define his identity and character.

It is precisely this simplicity that lies at the very heart of the work. *The Indian* does not stem from an elaborate conceptual framework, but from an image that struck me at that moment and which I felt the desire to translate into the material. It is one of those works in which instinct precedes explanation.

In the collective imagination, the figure of the Indian embodies an idea of ancient wisdom and knowledge gained through the observation of nature. It is this aspect, even more so than the ethnographic representation of the character, that has fuelled my fascination.

As one of my earliest works, the piece also retains a more immediate and spontaneous quality compared to my later explorations. The gesture is direct and the language still essential. Precisely for this reason, *The Indian* can also be seen as a testament to the origins of my artistic journey: not so much a manifesto of what was to come, but rather a trace of that early period in which images, impressions and personal impulses began to transform into painting.

Not everything an artist creates necessarily belongs to a single thematic trajectory. There are works born simply of the desire to capture an image, a sensation or a fascination. *L’Indiano* belongs to this freer and more instinctive aspect of my output.



**Technique and materials**

Work created in 2009 on a 0.5 cm thick Masonite panel, using plaster and acrylic paints. The minimalist technique and the simplified construction of the figure reflect the direct and spontaneous nature of the work.
