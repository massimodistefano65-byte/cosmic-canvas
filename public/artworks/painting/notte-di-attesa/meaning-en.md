
*"There are nights when time seems to stand still. Nothing has happened yet, and yet what is to come is already within us."*


**Description of the work**

*Night of Waiting* stems from a desire to portray waiting not simply as an interval between one moment and the next, but as a genuine inner state. It is that particular dimension in which the present seems to stretch out whilst something – whether desired or feared – slowly draws nearer.

The night amplifies this sensation. When everything around us slows down and the world seems to stand still, what lies within us becomes more evident. Waiting can then take on completely different forms: it may be the excitement of a journey due to begin the following day, the hope of receiving long-awaited news, the anticipation of a meeting, or, conversely, the fear of what is inevitably about to happen.

It is precisely this ambivalence that lies at the heart of the work. *Night of Waiting* does not recount a specific event nor does it determine whether what is about to happen is positive or negative. The night remains suspended and the viewer is invited to fill that void with their own experience, with their own expectations and with what, in their own life, they have learnt to wait for.

The moon plays a central role in creating this atmosphere. Its luminous presence emerges from the depths of the surface and seems to observe a scene in which time is momentarily suspended. Around it, the material has been worked to create textures and irregularities that disrupt the surface and allow the tension contained within the image to be physically felt.

Cobalt blue and earth tones create an intense nocturnal atmosphere, whilst gold leaf introduces sudden flecks of light. Gold does not necessarily represent something positive or a certain promise: it may be hope, desire, possibility, but also something distant that we cannot yet reach. Its presence in the night makes the contrast between what we know and what we are awaiting even more evident.

The textural surface further contributes to this sensation. The irregularities, marks and variations created during the working process react to natural light and alter the perception of the work as the day progresses. Light strikes the gold leaf, penetrates the textures and produces varying shadows, bringing out details that are never quite the same.

Waiting, after all, is precisely this: living in the present whilst a part of oneself is already looking towards something that does not yet exist. It is a state that can encompass both hope and fear, desire and unease.

One can await something one deeply desires with the same intensity as one might await something one fears. Even those who know their fate and realise that the following day will change their lives forever experience a night of waiting. Time remains the same, but the way it is perceived changes completely.

*Night of Waiting* deliberately leaves this possibility open. The night does not reveal what will happen. It merely suggests that **something is about to happen**.


**Technique and materials**

Work created in 2010 using mixed media on canvas. The composition utilises gold leaf, coloured earth pigments, cobalt blue and acrylic paints. The surface has been worked to create texture and material irregularities, which are particularly evident in the area surrounding the moon. The physical presence of the material interacts with the light, producing variations in shadows and reflections on the surface of the work.
