
*"Sometimes the mind doesn’t stop working: it simply can no longer make sense of what it produces."*

**Description of the work**

*Rotten Brain* stems from a desire to depict a particular moment of mental disarray, one in which the brain seems to be temporarily ‘out of action’: overloaded, disoriented, unable to clearly organise the multitude of thoughts passing through it. The title, deliberately direct and provocative, introduces with a touch of irony a condition that can be experienced by anyone going through a period of confusion or mental fatigue.

The surface of the work evokes a sort of distorted cerebral landscape. The numerous grooves and the lines that radiate and intersect seem to form a network of mental pathways that constantly overlap, without finding a precise direction. This is not an anatomical representation of a brain, but its translation into an abstract, almost organic material.

Bronze, turquoise, black and ochre produce a complex chromatic ensemble in which different sensations coexist. The metallic and earthy tones suggest something ancient, oxidised and worn, whilst the turquoise introduces a livelier, almost electric element. Black acts as an element of depth, accentuating the fractures and the denser areas of the composition.

The relationship between paint and material is particularly important. Polystyrene has not been used merely as a support, but has been physically transformed through heat. The surface has been hollowed out, deformed and shaped, creating bas-reliefs and high reliefs that lend the work a tangible three-dimensional structure. The grooves thus become an integral part of the visual language, almost as if they were traces left directly by the mental process being depicted.

The title *Rotten Brain* can also be interpreted ironically. That ‘rotten brain’ does not necessarily indicate something that is irrevocably compromised, but rather a temporary condition in which normal thought processes seem to jam. It is the moment when connections continue to exist, but no longer seem to lead to an orderly conclusion.

The work therefore forms part of my exploration of the inner world and the complexity of the mind, but does so through a rougher, more physical and almost corrosive language. The deformed material becomes a metaphor for the mind under strain: not necessarily destroyed, but altered, overloaded and momentarily unable to regain its balance.

*Rotten Brain* thus represents one of those moments when even thinking can become exhausting. A moment in which the mind continues to produce forms, connections and impulses, but temporarily loses the ability to organise them.

**Technique and materials**

Work created in 2020 using mixed media on polystyrene. The surface was treated with heat, creating a three-dimensional structure composed of bas-reliefs, high reliefs and deep grooves. The subsequent application of bronze, turquoise, black and ochre colours accentuates the material complexity and organic character of the composition.
