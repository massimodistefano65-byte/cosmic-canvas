*"Sometimes all it takes is a deep breath to reconnect with what the mind has forgotten amidst the clamour of everyday life."*



**Description of the work**

*Deep Breath* stems from a simple, primal gesture: pausing, breathing and re-establishing a connection with nature. Here, breathing becomes much more than a physiological act; it is a way of temporarily silencing the clamour of thoughts and rediscovering a more authentic perception of oneself and the environment around us.

The composition suggests a landscape without actually describing it. A sloping horizon line crosses the surface, hinting at the possibility of a sea, a stretch of water, perhaps a sunrise or a sunset. Yet nothing is entirely defined. The image remains suspended, like a perception observed through a sort of veil, leaving it to the viewer’s gaze to complete it.

The reference to nature does not take on an idyllic dimension here. The atmosphere appears dense, almost heavy, and the light fails to completely dispel the darkness that surrounds it. It is precisely this ambivalence that brings the work closer to the real experience of breathing and inner reconnection: nature can be welcoming, but not necessarily reassuring; it can offer a space of tranquillity even when tensions, anxieties and unresolved thoughts remain within us.

Deep breathing then becomes a form of passage. It does not necessarily serve to erase what weighs upon us, but to create the space needed to observe it without being completely overwhelmed by it. Just as when we stand before a landscape after a moment of silence, something within us slows down and gradually begins to perceive what was previously hidden by the noise.

The slanted horizon helps to disrupt the traditional balance of the scene. There is no perfect stability, nor a completely secure point of reference. Yet, it is precisely within this instability that the possibility of a pause emerges. The landscape thus becomes a sort of threshold between the outer and inner worlds, between what we see and what we feel.

*Deep Breath* therefore tells of a reconnection that arises not from escape, but from presence. To pause, breathe and look at nature means granting oneself the chance to return, at least for a few moments, to a more essential dimension of existence. Even when the landscape is not bright and reassuring, it can continue to fulfil its deepest function: to remind us that we are part of something that exists beyond the noise of our minds.



**Technique and materials**

Work created using mixed media on canvas. The layering of paint and the blending of tones create a soft, atmospheric surface, in which light, water and the horizon merge into a deliberately undefined and suspended perception.
