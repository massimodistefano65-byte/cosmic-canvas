Sometimes an image is not intentionally sought.
It emerges slowly from matter, light, and perception, like a hidden presence waiting to be recognized.

**The meaning of the artwork**
*"Gesù"* is born from an instinctive research into matter and the transformation of the pictorial surface.
The work was not conceived as a traditional figurative representation, but during an exhibition, an observer suddenly recognized the iconographic face of Christ in the composition.
From that moment, others also began to perceive that presence emerging through the shadows, lights, and stratifications of the matter.
This episode profoundly transformed the meaning of the work for me.
I understood that the image did not belong only to the painting, but also to the gaze and the perceptual sensitivity of the observer.
The figure in fact appears suspended between presence and dissolution: not completely defined, but in continuous emergence from the matter itself.
As if the face were not painted, but slowly revealed through the surface.
The work therefore speaks of the possibility that some archetypal images already exist within the collective memory and can spontaneously resurface through apparently abstract forms, shadows, and energies.
*"Gesù"* represents for me the mystery of the apparition:
the moment in which matter seems to become a vehicle for a deeper inner and symbolic presence.

**Technique and material**
Created in 2010 with mixed media, the work is born through stratifications of glues, plaster, acrylics, and sprays worked through palette knife strokes and direct interventions on the surface.
The dense textures, reliefs, and luminous variations generate a living and irregular matter, capable of continuously modifying the perception of the image depending on the light and the point of observation.
The figure thus emerges from the surface in a non-definitive way, reinforcing the sense of spontaneous apparition and hidden presence within the matter.
