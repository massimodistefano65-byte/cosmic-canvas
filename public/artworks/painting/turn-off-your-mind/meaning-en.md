
*"Sometimes true silence does not lie in having no thoughts, but in managing, at least for a moment, not to follow them."*


**Description of the work**

*Turn off your mind* stems from the need to represent that moment when the mind, following a continuous accumulation of thoughts, images, memories and connections, seeks to halt its ceaseless activity. The title should not be understood as an invitation to relinquish consciousness, but rather as the quest for a temporary suspension of mental noise.

The surface of the work is criss-crossed by a dense network of incisions, grooves and cavities created through deep hot-working of polystyrene. The material has been literally hollowed out and transformed, to the point of losing its original uniformity and becoming a complex surface, marked by paths and tensions.

This technical choice takes on particular significance in relation to the title. Thoughts can be interrupted, silenced or allowed to flow without being followed, but they rarely disappear completely. Just as the incisions remain in the material, so too do experiences and thoughts leave traces in the mind.

The composition therefore does not suggest absolute stillness. On the contrary, it appears to be in an intermediate state, suspended between movement and its interruption. It is as though the work represents the precise moment when the mind attempts to flick a switch and escape, at least temporarily, from its own flow.

The chromatic variations, together with the depth of the engravings, accentuate this sensation. Light enters the grooves, is refracted on the surfaces and creates a succession of shadows and reliefs that changes depending on the lighting and the viewing angle. The material is therefore not static: it reacts to light and conveys a slightly different perception each time.

In this sense, *Turn off your mind* can also be interpreted as a reflection on inner exploration. Switching off the mind does not mean destroying it, but learning, at least for a moment, not to be swept away by everything it produces.

The work thus depicts a form of silence that is difficult to attain: **not the absence of thought, but the suspension of its ceaseless dominance over consciousness.**


**Technique and materials**

Work created in 2013 using mixed media on polystyrene. The surface was deeply engraved and shaped using a heat-treatment process, creating grooves, cavities and reliefs directly within the material. The subsequent application of colour enhances the resulting texture and, through its interaction with light, accentuates the depth and complexity of the surface.
