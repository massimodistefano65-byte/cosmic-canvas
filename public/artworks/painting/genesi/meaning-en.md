
*"Every beginning already holds within it the possibility of transformation."*

**Description of the work**

*Genesi* dates from the early years of my exploration of painting and stems from an interest in the moment when something seems to take shape within matter. The image presents an apparently compact surface, traversed by a deep opening that suggests a gash, a fracture or a passage towards what lies beneath.

It is not necessary to determine precisely what this opening represents. It may be interpreted as a portion of earth splitting open, as a primordial substance from which something is emerging, or as a more inner dimension. It is precisely this ambiguity that leaves room for the viewer’s imagination.

The title *Genesi* thus evokes a process of origin and formation. It is not so much the depiction of a specific event as the idea of a moment in which a still-undefined form begins to manifest itself. The surface seems to harbour something that is not yet fully visible, as if the material itself contained a hidden possibility.

In this work, technique plays a particularly important role. The combined use of vinyl glues, acrylics and enamels allows for the creation of a surface rich in variations, contrasts and chromatic transitions. The paint is not merely a coating on the panel, but contributes to the very perception of the image, lending credibility to the sensation of a surface that opens up, offering a glimpse of an underlying reality.

The contrast between the outer surface and what appears through the slit creates a visual tension that also becomes the work’s main narrative element. The eye is naturally drawn into the opening and prompts the viewer to wonder what might lie beyond that surface.

As one of my works from 2010, *Genesi* also retains the spontaneity of the early phase of my artistic practice. It is a work in which the direct relationship with the material and the painterly gesture precedes a more defined conceptual structure. Precisely for this reason, the meaning is not fixed, but remains open to different interpretations.

*Genesi* can thus be viewed simply as the representation of a material that opens up, or as a metaphor for any process in which something, from a still indistinct state, slowly begins to manifest itself.

**Technique and materials**

Work created in 2010 using mixed media on panel, employing vinyl glues, acrylic paints and enamels. The combination of the different materials contributes to the construction of the surface, the chromatic contrasts and the definition of the central gash that characterises the composition.
