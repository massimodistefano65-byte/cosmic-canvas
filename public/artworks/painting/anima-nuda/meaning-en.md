
*"When all forms of protection fall away, what remains is an essential, fragile and irreducibly mysterious presence."*

**Description of the work**

*Anima nuda* depicts a deeply stylised human figure, set against a dark, irregular textured surface. The deliberately altered proportions, the elongated head and the extremely long fingers distance the figure from real anatomy, lending it an almost alien, enigmatic appearance, straddling the boundary between the human and the unknown.

The figure appears devoid of any element that might definitively define its identity. It is not a portrait, nor is it even a precise representation of a particular being: rather, it is a presence reduced to its very essence, as if it were what remains when everything that normally clothe and protects the individual is stripped away.

It is precisely this condition that gives meaning to the title *Anima nuda*. The figure seems to stand before itself, stripped of all masks, certainties or forms of protection. The unsettling aspect stems not only from its anatomical distortion, but above all from the vulnerability it conveys.

The hands, with their unusually long fingers, take on a particularly significant role. Their gesture seems to suggest a search, almost an attempt to reach or grasp something that lies beyond the figure. It is not an overtly defined gesture, but it conveys a yearning for something that is missing: a foothold, an answer, a presence, or perhaps a dimension different from the one in which the being finds itself.

The elongated shape of the head and the figure’s almost alien appearance also introduce a sense of otherness. The being depicted seems to belong simultaneously to something profoundly human and to something we cannot quite recognise. It is precisely in this ambiguity that the work finds its strength.

The dark, heavily textured surface further accentuates the sense of unease. The material seems to envelop the figure and, at the same time, cause it to emerge from an undefined environment. The black colour and the irregularities of the surface create an almost primordial atmosphere, within which the figure appears to be isolated and suspended.

*Anima nuda* thus connects to the artist’s inner exploration, but also to her attraction to that which cannot be immediately explained. The figure may be perceived as an image of the human being in all their vulnerability, but also as a presence emerging from an unknown dimension. No answer is provided: the question remains as to what might exist beyond what we are accustomed to recognising.

**Technique and materials**

Mixed media on polystyrene, created using **sand, plaster, glues and acrylic paints**.

The surface is built up through the accumulation and layering of materials, creating an irregular and highly textural surface. The sand contributes to the surface’s roughness and its distinctive tactile quality, whilst the plaster and other materials allow for variations in relief and depth.

The subsequent application of acrylics evens out and transforms the material, causing the figure to emerge from the dark background. The technique thus contributes directly to the atmosphere of the work: the surface is not merely a support, but becomes an integral part of the sense of isolation, vulnerability and mystery.
