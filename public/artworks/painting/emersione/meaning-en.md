There exist deep forces that remain invisible for a long time, moving slowly beneath the surface until the moment they begin to emerge, deforming matter and transforming what seemed immobile.

**The meaning of the artwork**
*"Emersione"* is born from the idea of a primordial force in the process of surfacing.
Something that slowly takes shape in the depths and that, through continuous pressure, finally manages to manifest itself in the visible world.
The vertical structure of the work appears as living matter in transformation: an organic presence that emerges from the darkness, crossed by tensions, openings, and stratifications that recall terrestrial fractures, roots, mountain ranges, or organisms in formation.
I was interested in evoking the exact moment in which a hidden energy begins to reveal itself.
It is not a reassuring or peaceful emergence, but a powerful, inevitable, almost geological process.
The work suggests the movement of something growing from the depths:
like the earth's crust rising, like a mass taking shape under pressure, or like inner aspects of consciousness that remain submerged for years before manifesting.
The contrast between the black of the surface and the light central matter further accentuates this sensation of apparition from the void and darkness.
*"Emersione"* therefore speaks of transformation as a natural and inner force, of that which slowly builds up in the depths until it becomes a visible presence.

**Technique and material**
Created on a polystyrene panel, the work is built exclusively through the addition and direct modeling of the matter.
The surface is born from intense manual shaping work using vinyl glues and processing tools that progressively sculpt the central organic structure, creating reliefs, tensions, and ramifications that recall natural or geological formations.
The matter is worked like a body in transformation, letting cracks, thickenings, and deformations emerge that accentuate the sensation of growth and internal pressure.
The essential chromatic intervention, dominated by the contrast between light and darkness, amplifies the primordial character of the work and concentrates attention on the very process of the form's emergence from the depth of the matter.
