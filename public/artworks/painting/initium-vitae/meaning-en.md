
*"The universe is constantly changing, and what appears to be new may simply be a form we have not yet learnt to recognise."*

**Description of the work**

*Initium Vitae* is a work characterised by powerful visual energy and an extremely intricate surface, in which earthy colours, deep tones and accents reminiscent of volcanic landscapes create a dimension that is both primordial and cosmic.

The image does not depict a specific place, but seems to offer a glimpse of something that is emerging and taking shape. The masses, the reliefs and the profound variations in the surface suggest an environment still in the process of transformation, in which it is impossible to determine with certainty where the landscape ends and something new begins.

The particular way the polystyrene has been worked contributes significantly to this sensation. The large-scale bulges and deformations created by heat transform the surface into an almost three-dimensional structure, in which the material appears to have been altered by an internal process. The result is reminiscent of natural formations, planetary surfaces and landscapes belonging to a world as yet unknown.

However, the primordial nature of the work should not be understood solely in a physical sense. *Initium Vitae* can be interpreted as a metaphor for the continuous transformation of existence: something that changes, organises itself and gradually takes on a form without it being possible to know in advance what the final result will be.

It is precisely this openness that brings the work particularly close to Massimo Di Stefano’s research. The image may suggest the origin of a world, the emergence of a new form of life or even the formation of an environment belonging to an extraterrestrial reality. None of these possibilities, however, is imposed as a definitive interpretation.

The cosmic dimension therefore remains inseparable from the deeper dimension of transformation. What appears to be a primordial surface can be perceived as a passage, a moment of transition in which a reality is taking on a new configuration.

*Initium Vitae* thus does not merely narrate a possible beginning, but above all **the state of transformation through which something may manifest itself in a form as yet unknown**. It is an image suspended between nature, the universe and the imagination, in which the mystery is not resolved but remains an essential part of the work.

**Technique and materials**

Work created in 2013 using mixed media on polystyrene. The surface has been deeply sculpted using **heat**, resulting in significant raised areas, distortions and variations in relief.

This process transforms the panel into a highly three-dimensional and irregular surface, upon which the painterly intervention creates a range of deep, earthy tones, with effects that evoke volcanic environments, natural formations and primordial landscapes.

The technique thus becomes an integral part of the work’s expression: it does not merely create a texture, but physically produces that sense of transformation and energy that characterises the entire composition.
