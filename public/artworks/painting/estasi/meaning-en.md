*"Ecstasy does not always manifest itself in light. Sometimes it arises in silence, in the contrast between what appears and what remains hidden."*



**Description of the work**

*Ecstasy* stems from the exploration of an inner state that is difficult to represent through an explicitly narrative image. The ecstasy evoked by the title is not a state of euphoria or obvious abandon, but a more intimate and silent moment, in which perception seems to drift away for an instant from the ordinary realm.

The work builds this tension through a minimalist composition, dominated exclusively by black and gold. Two profoundly different chromatic elements meet on the surface, creating a relationship between darkness and light, depth and revelation, matter and perception.

Black introduces a dimension of mystery and inner life. It is not simply an absence of light, but seems to open up a space into which the gaze can immerse itself. Gold, by contrast, captures the light and reflects it back across the surface, taking on an almost symbolic presence. Its preciousness is not merely aesthetic: it can be interpreted as something rare emerging from the darkness.

The plaster finish lends the panel a material quality. The surface is not a neutral plane, but a modified structure, marked and prepared to receive the gold leaf. In this way, the light of the gold interacts with the very material of the work, ensuring that the visual experience also changes depending on the ambient light and the vantage point.

It is precisely in this contrast that the ‘ecstasy’ of the title can be grasped. Not an explosion, not a spectacular display, but **a moment of suspension**, in which something seems to emerge from the silence and take on a different presence.

The work suggests that the deepest states of consciousness are not necessarily accompanied by spectacular images. Sometimes an intense perception can manifest itself through a form reduced to its essence, leaving the viewer the necessary space to complete it internally.

*Ecstasy* thus becomes a reflection on the possibility of perceiving something beyond the immediate surface of things. The gold does not simply illuminate the black: it seems to open up, within itself, a possibility of transcendence. And ecstasy, in this sense, is not so much what the work shows, as **what may occur in the gaze of those who observe it closely**.



**Technique and materials**

The work is created using mixed media on panel. The surface was prepared and treated with gesso and subsequently enhanced by applying gold leaf. The composition is characterised exclusively by the interplay between black and gold, creating a strong contrast in texture and light.
