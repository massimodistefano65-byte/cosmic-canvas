*"In a dream, forms do not yet need to be defined. They exist first and foremost as perceptions, intuitions and presences."*



**Description of the work**

*Dreamlike Experience* stems from a reflection on dreams as a dimension distinct from ordinary reality, a space in which images, sensations and thoughts can take on forms that do not necessarily follow the logic of everyday life. The dream world has always held an important place in my inner exploration: the night and dreams become places of exploration, where consciousness seems able to temporarily transcend its usual boundaries.

At the centre of the composition emerges a large, spherical form, undefined and suspended. It does not represent anything specific and does not yet appear to be fully formed. Rather, it is a presence in the making, something that seems to be slowly emerging within a dimension in which the contours of reality lose their substance.

Around this form, a blurred and nebulous chromatic mass unfolds. The colours do not construct a recognisable landscape, but generate a sort of perceptual atmosphere. It is as though the image does not seek to represent a place within a dream, but **the very sensation of being within a dreamlike experience**.

This indefiniteness is an essential part of the work. In dreams, we often perceive something even before we are able to identify it. An image can seem both familiar and unfamiliar at the same time; a presence can be clearly perceived without having a precise form. *Dreamlike Experience* seeks to convey precisely this state of limbo, in which perception precedes understanding.

The materials also play a decisive role in this experience. Enamels, earth pigments and vinyl glues have been used to create thickness, irregularities and ripples on the surface of the canvas. The painting is therefore not confined to a two-dimensional plane, but acquires a physical texture that interacts directly with light.

The surface irregularities generate shadows and chromatic variations that change depending on the direction and intensity of the lighting. The work can therefore appear different at various times of day, continually altering its presence. This is a particularly important feature of my textural works: light does not merely illuminate the surface, but becomes part of the work and helps to construct a different perception of it each time.

In this sense, the material of the work becomes almost a metaphor for the dream itself. Dreams, too, change as we experience them: some images emerge, others fade away; some remain etched in our minds whilst others slip from our memory immediately. Nothing is entirely stable.

*Dreamlike Experience* does not, therefore, seek to recount a specific dream. Rather, it seeks to evoke that particular moment in which consciousness enters a different dimension – one that is more fluid and less bound by the usual forms of reality. It is a space in which something seems to take shape without yet being fully defined, leaving the door open to many interpretations.



**Technique and materials**

Work created in 2017 using mixed media on canvas. The surface was treated with enamels, earth pigments and vinyl glues to create depth, texture and ripples. The textural irregularities interact with natural light, producing variations in shadow, depth and colour perception.
