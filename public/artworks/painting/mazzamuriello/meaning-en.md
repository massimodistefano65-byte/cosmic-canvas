
*"Between memory and mystery, a presence takes shape where reality gives way to the unknown."*

**Description of the work**

*Mazzamuriello* stems from a family memory linked to the stories told by the artist’s grandparents, who recounted episodes in which a mysterious presence—known in Neapolitan tradition as *Mazzamuriello*—is said to have appeared. Those stories, suspended between lived experience, folk memory and inexplicable phenomena, left a lasting impression on the artist that was destined to resurface in her artistic practice.

The work does not seek to represent a supernatural being in a literal sense. Rather, the central form appears as an indefinite, semi-organic presence that seems to emerge from the surface of the canvas. Its irregular contours, points and folds prevent it from being identified as a specific figure, leaving it deliberately suspended between organism, energy and apparition.

It is precisely this ambiguity that constitutes one of the work’s most interesting elements. What the *Mazzamuriello* is supposed to be is not shown; rather, the sensation of standing before something that does not entirely belong to known reality is evoked.

The strong three-dimensionality of the surface accentuates this impression. Indeed, the form does not simply appear to be painted, but rather **constructed and emerging from the material**, through overlays, folds, reliefs and distortions. The viewer is thus led to question not only the image, but the very nature of what they are observing.

The work ties in naturally with Massimo Di Stefano’s curiosity regarding paranormal phenomena, experiences that are difficult to explain, and the possibility of forms of existence that elude ordinary perception. It is a curiosity that also extends to an interest in life beyond the earthly realm and the possibility of extraterrestrial life forms: not as a certainty to be asserted, but as a question to be kept open.

In this sense, *Mazzamuriello* offers no explanation. Instead, it preserves that particular state that arises when a story, an experience or a presence cannot be fully accounted for by reason. The memory of family tales thus becomes artistic material, and the mystery, rather than being resolved, is left intact.

**Technique and materials**

Mixed media on canvas, created by layering **paper, plaster, vinyl glues, earth pigments and acrylic paints**.

The surface is constructed through a highly textural and three-dimensional process. Folds, flaps, reliefs and accumulations of material generate an irregular structure that contributes to the definition of the central form.

The material therefore does not serve a purely technical function, but becomes an integral part of the image: the subject seems to emerge physically from the surface through the overlapping layers, creating an effect that oscillates between relief, painting and sculptural form.
