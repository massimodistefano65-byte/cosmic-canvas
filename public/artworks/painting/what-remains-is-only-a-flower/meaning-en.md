When time dissolves forms, what remains is not the chronicle of an experience, but its essence.

**Description of the work**
*"What Remains Is Only a Flower"* is born from a reflection on memory and its inevitable transformation. Time modifies every lived experience: contours blur, events lose definition, and details slowly dissolve, letting only what possesses an authentic emotional value emerge.
Within a composition rich in signs, chromatic stratifications, and visual fragments, the delicate presence of a flower surfaces. It does not dominate the scene, but seems to emerge naturally from the flow of the pictorial matter, like a memory that resurfaces without being sought.
The flower becomes the symbol of what survives time: not the entire experience, but its most authentic part, the most fragile and at the same time the most precious. It is the emotional synthesis of a lived experience that continues to inhabit the memory despite having lost its original form.
The work suggests that memory does not faithfully preserve the past. It transforms it, distills it, until it retains only what continues to have a deep meaning. Sometimes, of a life, an encounter, or an emotion, what remains is truly only a flower.

**Technique and material**
The work is created on forex using anilines, a technique that allows colors to expand, overlap, and merge spontaneously, generating transparencies, glazes, and chromatic stratifications. The support contributes to enhancing the luminosity of the color fields and the lightness of the composition, reinforcing the evocative character of the work.
