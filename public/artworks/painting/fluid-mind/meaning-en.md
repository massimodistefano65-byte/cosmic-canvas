*"A truly free mind does not always follow a set path: sometimes it allows itself to flow into realms that reason cannot yet explain."*

**Description of the work**

*Fluid Mind* stems from the idea of representing a mind free to move beyond the usual paths of thought, without being necessarily bound by linear logic or pre-established patterns.

The work does not seek to depict the brain or thought literally, but rather to translate the sensation of a mind in constant motion into a visual and material dimension. The surface appears to be articulated by bas-reliefs, high-reliefs and incised lines that meet, break off and develop in different directions, creating a structure in which there is no single way of interpreting it.

This complexity suggests a mind that moves through associations, intuitions and digressions. A fluid mind can traverse familiar territory whilst, at the same time, allowing itself to be led towards darker and more mysterious realms, where answers are not immediate and rationality is no longer the only possible means of understanding.

The work’s almost psychedelic quality stems precisely from this openness. Thought seems to gradually lose its boundaries and transform into a perceptual experience in which reality, imagination and the inner dimension can overlap.

In this sense, *Fluid Mind* does not represent a mind that is simply ‘open’, but a mind capable of **transforming itself whilst exploring**. The flow thus also becomes a process of evolution: every path may lead to another, every deviation may open up an unexpected possibility, and even what appears obscure can become part of the quest.

The work thus forms part of Massimo Di Stefano’s body of work centred on inner exploration and the perception of reality, but it does so through a predominantly material and perceptual language. No answer is offered regarding the ultimate meaning of what is being explored; rather, it represents the freedom to continue seeking.

**Technique and materials**

Mixed media on polystyrene, worked mainly using heat to shape and deform the surface.

The thermal treatment allows for the creation of a complex structure of **bas-reliefs, high reliefs and carved lines**, transforming the original panel into a three-dimensional surface. The relief therefore does not serve a purely decorative function: it becomes the language through which the movement of thought is visualised.

The variations in depth, the incisions and the deformations generate an alternation of solid and void spaces that contributes to a sense of fluidity and continuous transformation. The material itself seems to follow a process analogous to that of the mind: it does not remain static, but is modified, traversed and transformed.
