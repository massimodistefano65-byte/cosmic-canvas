

*"The metropolis is a place where movement never stops, but precisely for this reason it can be difficult to find a space to breathe."*

**Description of the work**
*Metropolis* stems from a desire to depict the chaotic nature of the big city, where traffic, movement, noise and a jumble of stimuli transform the urban space into a reality constantly fraught with tension.
The composition conveys this sensation through a dense and dynamic painterly texture, in which forms appear to overlap and interfere with one another. What emerges is not the orderly, recognisable image of a city, but rather the perception of what a metropolis can evoke in the individual: congestion, unease and difficulty in finding a moment of tranquillity.
Chalk and paints help to create a surface that does not simply appear painted, but is permeated by a constant agitation. The material thus becomes an expressive tool capable of translating external disorder into an almost physical sensation.
In *Metropolis*, urban chaos is not merely a characteristic of the environment, but becomes a psychological condition. Traffic and ceaseless movement represent a reality in which the individual risks losing their inner space, overwhelmed by stimuli and presences that offer no respite.
The work thus also takes on a more personal meaning. The artist’s distance from metropolitan life and his choice to live immersed in the countryside stem precisely from the need to escape this excess of noise, confusion and tension, in order to reclaim a more direct relationship with silence, nature and his own inner world.
*Metropolis* therefore becomes the counterpoint to that quest for tranquillity which runs through many of the artist’s works. Whilst nature allows us to slow down and reconnect with ourselves, the metropolis depicted in this work seems to be moving in the opposite direction: everything is rushing, everything overlaps, everything demands attention.
The result is a depiction not so much of the city itself, but of the **sensation of living within a system that never stops moving**.

**Technique and materials**
The technique contributes directly to the construction of the work’s meaning. The use of plaster and the layering of paints create a dense, textured surface, accentuating the sensation of accumulation, congestion and movement. The material thus becomes an integral part of the depiction of urban chaos, transforming the canvas into a space in which traces seem to continually overlap and interfere with one another.
