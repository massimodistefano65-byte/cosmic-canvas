Confusion does not always arise from the absence of answers. Sometimes it arises from an excess of possibilities.

**Description of the work**
*"Stato confusionale"* explores that mental condition in which thoughts, emotions, and perceptions overlap until it becomes difficult to distinguish what is essential from what is merely noise.
The work does not simply represent confusion: it constructs it before the viewer's eyes. The images layer, cross, and contaminate each other, creating a perceptual depth that continuously changes depending on the point of observation. What appears clear from one perspective becomes uncertain from another, just as happens in moments when the mind struggles to find orientation.
Despite the sense of bewilderment it suggests, the work does not convey a negative vision. Confusion becomes a phase of the transformation process, an intermediate territory in which old certainties dissolve before new balances can emerge. It is a necessary suspension, a passage that belongs to the human experience and the continuous search for self.

**Technique and material**
The work is created on cardstock with a particular layering of transparent acetate sheets, each individually painted with acrylics and anilines. The layering of the different levels generates a three-dimensional effect and a visual depth that changes with the viewing angle, making the technique an integral part of the work's meaning.
The work is offered complete with a frame with passe-partout, for a final dimension of 52 × 42 cm. The dimensions of the work on cardstock and acetate sheets alone are 30 × 40 cm.
