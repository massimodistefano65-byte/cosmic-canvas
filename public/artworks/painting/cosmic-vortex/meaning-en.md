
*"Every vortex leads towards a centre, but every centre can be the beginning of a new movement."*


**Description of the work**

*Cosmic Vortex* is one of the artist’s earliest works in which their exploration of a dimension that unites matter, nature and the cosmic imagination is particularly evident. The composition is dominated by a large spiral-like movement that develops around a centre and gradually expands outwards.

The form immediately brings to mind a vortex, but also a shell, a galaxy viewed from above, or an energy current coiling upon itself. It is precisely this ambiguity that makes the image so intriguing: a natural form can be transformed in the imagination into a cosmic structure, whilst an apparently cosmic structure can lead back once more to the elementary forms of nature.

The centre of the spiral does not simply appear as a point of arrival. Rather, it is a centre of attraction from which the movement seems to arise and towards which, at the same time, it seems to tend. The spiral thus becomes a representation of continuous movement, in which the interior and exterior are not truly separate.

The term *Cosmic* further broadens this perception. The vortex can be interpreted as a metaphor for the universe, for matter and energy moving in circular and spiral-like patterns. But the same structure can also be observed on a more intimate level: consciousness, inner exploration and thought rarely proceed in a straight line. They often return to the same themes, but each time from a different vantage point, like a spiral that seems to retrace its path without ever truly being in the same place.

The technique helps to give tangible form to this movement. Vinyl glues, earth pigments and acrylics build a surface in which gesture and material follow the dynamics of the composition. The vortex therefore does not merely appear to be depicted: it almost seems to have formed through a process of accumulation and transformation of the material.

The spiral form also evokes one of the most recurring geometric shapes in the natural world. The seashell, the growth of certain biological structures, galaxies and many atmospheric phenomena all display, in different ways, configurations that can be traced back to the movement of the spiral. In the artwork, these references are not described scientifically, but come together in an intuitive and symbolic image.

*Cosmic Vortex* can therefore be viewed simultaneously as a natural form, a cosmic vortex and a representation of the journey of consciousness. An inward movement which, just as it penetrates the centre, seems to open out towards an infinitely larger space.


**Technique and materials**

Work created in 2010 using mixed media on panel. The composition was created using vinyl glues, earth pigments and acrylic paints, combined to construct and define the spiral-like movement and the distinctive textural structure of the surface.
