*When the mind transcends its own limits, the outside world is transformed into a labyrinth of signs, sounds and possibilities.*

**Description of the work**

“My Mind Is Over” presents an urban space transformed by an altered and intensified perception. The image appears to depict a real place, but its structure is permeated by distortions, flashes of light and lines that profoundly alter its legibility.

The scene appears to be a nocturnal urban setting, rich in signs, architecture, surfaces and traces of light. However, the radial movement that emanates from the centre and spreads out towards the edges renders the space unstable, almost as if reality were being pulled off its axis.

The centre of the work retains a greater concentration of detail. Here, one can make out structures, signs and fragments of an urban environment, but their presence is constantly disrupted by overlaps and interference. The white and coloured lines seem to criss-cross the landscape like thoughts, electrical impulses or connections that multiply without ever reaching a conclusion.

The cool colours, dominated by blue, turquoise, black and grey, are punctuated by flashes of pink, red and purple. This contrast creates a nocturnal yet far from silent atmosphere: the city appears brimming with signals, stimuli and information. The lights do not simply illuminate the surroundings, but seem to amplify their complexity.

The title ‘My Mind Is Over’ can be interpreted as the expression of a mind that has reached the limit of its capacity to contain what it is experiencing. The phrase does not necessarily indicate a definitive conclusion, but rather a state of saturation: too many thoughts, too many images and too many impulses overlapping at the same time.

The distortion of perspective makes this very experience visible. The space seems to rush towards the observer and, at the same time, recede. It is as though the mind can no longer maintain an orderly distance from things. The external world becomes a reflection of an inner overload.

Yet the work does not convey confusion alone. Within the complexity of the signs, there also lies a form of creative energy. The lines, lights and overlays construct a new perceptual order, distinct from the realistic one but not devoid of structure.

‘My Mind Is Over’ thus represents a critical threshold of consciousness: the moment when thought transcends its own boundaries and perception transforms into a continuous flow. The mind seems to be at its limit, but it is precisely from that limit that a new, more intense and less controllable vision of reality emerges.

**Technique and materials**

This work is created using **Digital Art**, through the digital manipulation and superimposition of urban images, motion effects, perspective distortions, lines of light and chromatic interventions.

Digital processing transforms real space into an unstable perceptual surface, in which radial movement and graphic interference reveal the mind’s overload and the proliferation of stimuli.
