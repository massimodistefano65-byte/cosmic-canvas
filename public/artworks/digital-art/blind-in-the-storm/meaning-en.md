
*"When everything around us is in flux, the difficulty lies not in weathering the storm, but in still being able to see a way forward."*


**Description of the work**

*Blind in the Storm* arises from the depiction of a state of profound disorientation, in which space seems to lose all stability and perception is overwhelmed by constant movement.

At the centre of the image emerges an almost human presence, fragmented and partially submerged by graphic overlays. It does not appear as a clear, recognisable figure: rather, it seems to be a presence attempting to maintain its own identity whilst everything around it is distorted, traversed and broken down.

Red dominates the composition with an immediate force. It is an intense, vivid, almost physical red, which contrasts violently with the white and black. It does not appear as a mere chromatic element, but as a force that cuts through the scene: energy, alarm, tension, a vital impulse and, at the same time, danger. The white, on the other hand, bursts forth through a dense web of lines that open outwards, creating the impression of a violent and unstable light.

The entire composition seems built around a centrifugal, whirling movement. The lines, spirals and circular forms converge and diverge simultaneously, creating a sensation of acceleration. Space is no longer a place in which the figure is situated, but becomes the storm itself.

It is precisely in this relationship between the central figure and the vortex that the title takes on its deepest meaning. *Blind in the Storm* means being blind within the storm: not necessarily deprived of sight, but unable, for a moment, to discern a direction, a fixed point, or a way out. Blindness thus becomes a perceptual and existential condition.

The central figure seems to be seeking some form of orientation, but is continually intersected by signs, fragments, lines and overlaps. The face, where its presence can be perceived, appears almost as something that is emerging and disappearing at the same time. It is a presence that resists dissolution.

The chromatic contrast further intensifies this tension. Black introduces depth and density, white opens up sudden areas of glare, whilst red acts as a pulsation running through the entire work. None of the three colours, however, manages to definitively prevail over the others: their collision keeps the image in a state of constant instability.

The storm can therefore also be interpreted as an inner state. There are moments when thoughts, emotions, fears and impulses overlap to the point where it becomes difficult to distinguish what is essential from what is merely noise. In those moments, the individual may feel like the figure depicted in the work: immersed in the movement, unable to see clearly, yet still present at the centre of their own experience.

*Blind in the Storm* does not, therefore, depict a storm observed from the outside. It takes us directly into it. Its power stems precisely from the impossibility of immediately finding a point of stillness: the work compels the gaze to move, to lose its way and to seek a centre once more.

It is in this search that the ‘blindness’ of the title can be transformed into an opportunity. When our usual vision fails, what remains is not necessarily a void: it may be the beginning of another form of perception.


**Technique and materials**

Work created in 2016 within the field of **Digital Art**. The composition utilises overlapping forms, lines, marks and circular structures, constructed through digital processing heavily focused on the layering and distortion of the image. The combination of red, black and white, together with the dynamic treatment of the forms, produces the effect of a vortex, fragmentation and movement that characterises the work.
