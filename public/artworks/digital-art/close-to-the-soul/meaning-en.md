*A dizzying descent into the very core of being, where the fear of the void yields to the light of truth.*

**Description of the work**

Visually, the work appears as a magnetic abyss, a concentric vortex in which the cool, distant tones of teal and slate plunge inexorably towards a red, incandescent core. But for the artist, this rotational geometry is not a mere perceptual exercise: it is the desperate and necessary excavation towards the centre of the self. 

As postulated by his artistic exploration, Di Stefano does not create to decorate, but to probe. *Close to the soul* represents that primordial spiritual hunger, the alchemical drive of those seeking authentic contact, a ‘mirror that does not lie’. The vibrating core at the centre is not simply a cosmic sun, but the ‘living flesh’ of the soul, the exact point where the artist’s inner urgency refuses to be silenced and demands to be seen. In this movement of implosion and explosion, the work challenges the fear of insignificance and emptiness, transforming anguish into an absolute, pulsating presence. It is an invitation to inhabit one’s own boundaries, to allow oneself to be deciphered right down to the bone without losing one’s integrity.

**Technique and materials**

Through digital art, Di Stefano bends the cold medium of the pixel to his searing introspective will. He does not use the machine to simulate reality, but to capture a vibration that would otherwise be invisible. Digitally generated micro-fissures and optical reliefs become the translation of a fractured inner world, demonstrating that even in the immaterial realm of the digital, light can penetrate and transfigure the substance of the soul.
