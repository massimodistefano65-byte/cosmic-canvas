*"Sometimes awakening does not consist in seeing something new, but in seeing for the first time what was already before our eyes."*



**Description of the work**

*Awakening in Kyoto* stems from the encounter between nature, perception and transformation. The image depicts a woodland setting bathed in intense, almost unreal light, in which the leaves seem to detach themselves from the natural world to become luminous presences suspended in space.

The first element to catch the eye is precisely the contrast between the dark backdrop of the woodland and the warmth of the leaves. The deep greens, blacks and shaded areas create a dense, intimate atmosphere, whilst the ochre, golden and orange tones of the leaves seem to glow from within. The light does not simply illuminate the woodland: it appears to emanate from a hidden source and pass right through it.

At the centre of the image lies an area of intense brightness, from which rays radiate outwards, passing through the vegetation. It is as though the natural space had suddenly been traversed by a perceptual event. Light thus becomes the true protagonist of the work.

The title *Awakening in Kyoto* introduces a specific yet non-descriptive dimension. Kyoto evokes a culture in which the relationship with nature, the changing of the seasons and the perception of the moment hold profound significance. In the work, however, this reference is not illustrated literally. Rather, it is evoked through a contemplative sensibility.

The ‘awakening’ of the title can therefore be understood as a shift in perspective. The woodland is still there, but something in our perception has changed. The sudden light reveals a dimension that normally remains hidden within the natural world.

The radial movement of the light also contributes to this sensation. The rays seem to expand from the centre outwards, as if a point of awareness had suddenly begun to spread through space. The image thus shifts from a simple observation of nature to an almost inner state.

The presence of the golden foliage also introduces the theme of time. The leaves are part of a natural cycle in which birth, transformation and decay are not separate moments, but parts of the same process. The beauty of the image arises precisely from this transience.

*Awakening in Kyoto* is therefore a work about the moment when everyday reality suddenly seems to take on a different depth. The world need not change: it may be enough simply for the way we look at it to change.



**Technique and materials**

Work created in 2011 within the field of **Digital Art**. The image combines photographic elements with digital processing techniques, through overlays, light transformations and colour manipulations. The treatment of light and the construction of radial rays transform the natural landscape into a vision suspended between photography, memory and perception.
