There are moments when nature seems to cross its visible boundaries and merge completely with color, light, and perception.
In that instant, reality stops being simple observation and becomes an inner experience.

**The meaning of the artwork**
*"Lost in Color"* is born from a sudden and deeply symbolic encounter that occurred during the creative process.
While I was painting, a mantis spontaneously landed on one of my works, as if for a moment nature and painting had stopped being separate worlds.
I perceived that instant as a suspension of time: a living presence completely immersed in the vibration of color.
The photograph does not aim to represent only an insect or a naturalistic detail, but the moment in which life seems to dissolve into the perceptual experience and become part of something broader.
The mantis appears almost absorbed by the chromatic surface, losing its defined contours until it merges with the movement of color and light.
In this dissolution of boundaries, a contemplative dimension emerges for me: the transition from an individual perception to a sensation of belonging to the whole.
The work therefore speaks of a deep connection between human being, nature, and creative energy.
A subtle ecstasy in which the heartbeat of life meets the vibration of matter and color.
*"Lost in Color"* represents the temporary abandonment of separation, the dissolving of identity within the living continuity of existence.

**Technique and perception**
Macro photography created through a close-up research of chromatic matter and natural presence.
The use of shallow depth of field and immersion in color transforms the scene into an almost abstract space, where subject and pictorial surface seem to merge into a single perceptual vibration.
The work was selected and presented as part of the XV International Exhibition of MacroPhotography at the Louvre, through a digital projection dedicated to the works considered among the most significant of the exhibition.
